# Constitution Builder — design

_2026-11-19. Status: agreed direction. Phase 1 (Draft + Form + taking office) built 2026-11-20._

## The idea in one line

Write the rules, form a government under them, then run experiments to see how that government behaves. The lab never grades the design. It shows consequences, and points to neutral real-world echoes.

## The loop

1. **Draft** — choose the rules (three levers to start, one card each).
2. **Form** — name the people and bodies your rules created, then hold a first election.
3. **Test** — run short experiments against your government (pass a bill, lose a majority, handle an emergency).
4. **Change one rule and run it again** — the same move Norland already teaches with its PM → President rewind.

Saving and sharing a constitution and its results comes later (see Phase 4), and the design leaves room for it.

## Where the visitor starts

The welcome screen goes straight into the builder with one action, **Start your constitution**. Norland moves off the first screen. It stays as Experiment 01 in the Main Lab, and its engine becomes the core of the builder's experiments.

## 1. Draft: the levers

Each lever is a card with 3–7 options, written in the same template as `explorer/head-of-government/data.json` (`id`, `label`, `mechanism`, `how_chosen`, `how_removed`, ...). Same card size and evidence length for every option, as that file's `neutrality_note` already requires.

| Lever | Options (first pass) | Reuse |
|---|---|---|
| Who runs the government | Parliamentary confidence · Direct presidential · Assembly-appointed · Dual executive · Collegial council · Ruling monarch · Ruling council or party | The 5 options in `data.json`, plus two non-democratic ones |
| Legislature | One chamber · Two equal chambers · Two chambers, weak upper house · Appointed advisory council · None | Matches the planned "One chamber or two?" module |
| Who chooses the legislature | Winner-takes-the-district · Proportional · Mixed · Limited franchise (e.g. property or citizenship class) · Appointed by the ruler · Chosen by lot | New |

Non-democratic designs are allowed from day one, in line with the README's scope (monarchies, oligarchies, theocracies, Athenian-style lot and assembly). They use the same card template and the same neutral tone as every other option.

We start with these three. Until more levers exist, the builder uses sensible defaults for everything else (for example, a directly elected president can issue time-limited decrees, as in Norland), and says so in one line.

Later levers, roughly in order: head of state, veto, emergency powers, judicial review, amendment rule, term limits, federal vs unitary.

Invalid or odd combinations are allowed. Instead of blocking them, the builder shows **"Watch for"** notes generated by rules in the existing `interaction_rules` format (`if` / `then` / `explanation` / `mitigations`). Example: direct presidential + proportional + one chamber → "Watch for: dual legitimacy and deadlock." These are explanations, not warnings or scores.

## 2. Form: name your government

The constitution decides which roles exist. A collegial council produces seven councillors and no prime minister; a monarchy produces a monarch; two chambers produce two chamber names.

For every role the visitor sees two fields, **title** and **name**, each pre-filled with a generic choice and a 🎲 button to draw another:

- Country: "Norland" or one of a pool of invented names.
- Head of government: title "Prime Minister" / "President" / "Chancellor" / "First Councillor", name from a pool of neutral, varied invented names (Dana Reyes stays in the pool).
- Head of state, legislature and chamber names ("Assembly", "Senate", "Council of Elders"), top court, two to four parties.
- Supporting cast reused from Norland: a chief of staff (Priya) and an opposition leader (Tom Hadley), renameable like everyone else.

Then the government **takes office**. How depends on the rules: an election, an appointment by the ruler, a succession, or a draw by lot. For elected legislatures, a small seat generator turns the electoral system into a parliament: winner-takes-the-district tends toward one big party, proportional tends toward several parties and a coalition. It uses a seeded random draw so "run it again" is reproducible, and the result is shown as a seat bar with the governing majority (or lack of one) labelled.

## 3. Test: experiments

Experiments reuse Norland's engine, generalised:

- The same state shape (`P` progress, `C` political capital, `U` legal exposure, `out`), the same meters, the same three-step choice structure and end-state tables.
- **Choices come from the constitution.** Norland's "Take the shortcut" already means a confidence vote for a PM and a decree for a President. In the builder, each choice lists which rules enable it and what it becomes under each, so the shortcut might be a confidence vote, a decree, a referendum, or simply unavailable (shown greyed out with one line on why).
- **Text is templated** with the names from step 2: `{hog.title} {hog.name}`, `{legislature.name}`, `{opposition.name}`.

First experiments, in build order:

1. **Pass the Bridge and Rail Act** — Norland's scenario, now played under the visitor's rules.
2. **Lose the majority** — a coalition partner walks out or a by-election goes badly.
3. **The flood** — an emergency that tempts the executive toward emergency powers (best once the emergency powers lever exists).
4. **The court case** — someone challenges a law (needs the judicial review lever).
5. **Succession** — the ruler dies or steps down. Mainly for monarchies and ruling councils, but every design has to answer "who's next?".

Non-democratic designs change what the experiments test. Where there's no legislature to win over, the pressures come from elsewhere: the ruling council, the army, regions, public unrest. Each experiment lists which pressures apply under which rules, the same way choices do.

## Real-world echoes, not judgements

After an experiment, an **Echoes** panel can show one or two real cases where a similar rule played out, in Norland's existing `CASES` style: what happened, then a **"What's different"** paragraph. Ground rules:

- An echo is attached to a rule or a combination, never to the visitor's whole constitution, and never says "your system is like country X".
- Where possible, pair a case where the rule held up with one where it struggled.
- No scores, rankings or verdicts anywhere in the builder. The outcome cards describe trade-offs ("Some call that stability. Others call it gridlock."), as Norland already does.
- Every case is sourced in `notes/`, and the invented parts are labelled as invented there. Each case links the constitutional clause (Constitute Project) and an established reference for what happened; see `docs/sourcing-policy.md`.

## How it's built

Same constraints as the rest of the site: static files, vanilla JS, no build step, no database or external API.

```
builder/index.html     # Draft → Form → Test, one page, three panels
builder/builder.js     # state, election, experiment engine
builder/data.json      # levers, roles, name pools, rules, experiments, echoes
```

The whole constitution is one plain object, which is what later makes saving easy:

```json
{
  "version": 1,
  "country": "Norland",
  "rules": { "hog": "parliamentary_confidence", "chambers": "two_weak_upper", "electoral": "proportional" },
  "actors": { "hog": { "title": "Prime Minister", "name": "Dana Reyes" }, "legislature": { "name": "Assembly" } },
  "seed": 4812
}
```

Progress is kept in `sessionStorage` during a visit (`constitutionlab.builder.v1`, separate from Norland's `constitutionlab.v1`).

## Phases

1. **Draft + Form.** Three levers, naming, taking office, "Watch for" notes. No experiments yet. Welcome screen points here.
2. **First experiment.** Bridge and Rail Act under the visitor's rules, plus "change one rule and rerun".
3. **More experiments and Echoes.** Lose the majority, the flood, the court case; echoes panel with sourced cases.
4. **Save and share.** `localStorage` "My constitutions" list, plus a share link that encodes the constitution in the URL. Both stay within the no-database rule. Saving results is the same object plus a list of experiment outcomes.

## Decided with John (2026-11-19)

- Norland comes off the welcome screen. The site opens on the builder.
- Non-democratic designs are available from day one.
- Start with three levers: who runs the government, the legislature, and who chooses it. Add more later.
